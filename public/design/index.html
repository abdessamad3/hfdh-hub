Updated 17 October 2026. Tap any screen above to open it full size.

Version 2 is fully designed and planned on Firebase's free plan; the specification can start now, and a few small tests and decisions must come before the feature steps.

In short

The v1 app was reviewed, fixed and kept live. v2 was decided, designed (21 screens, one look) and planned in ten steps. Recording, Mushaf marking and live tracking were studied in depth, and a test page for tracking is deployed.

  • Decided: several separate kuttabs, Warsh, teacher-only recording, four mistake marks (ن ت خ ص) kept in a history, admin-only permissions, free plan, no payment card.
  • Built so far: v1 fixes and automatic deploy; the recitation lab at its own address; the Full Design canvas (21 screens).
  • Learned: automatic tracking works on correct text but is too slow on a phone browser (13 s a step), so version 1 uses a teacher-driven highlight.
  • Next: step 0, the specification. It must settle four technical tests, a test Firebase project, and the Warsh text and licence before the feature steps.

How the pages connect

  • Roll call feeds attendance on the manager dashboard and reports, the parent's "today" and the student's streak.
  • Mushaf marks form the verse history shown to the student, the parent and the manager, and feed the mistake counts and the student card.
  • Recording clips attach to verse marks, so a student can play the exact mistake from the Mushaf; exam recordings appear on the student's home.
  • Planned tasks show as the student's next task; missed ones count on the manager's dashboard.
  • Manager settings drive everything else: the sessions in roll call, the archive limits that raise alerts, the points behind the ranking, clip lengths and retention, and which events notify parents.
  • An archive alert becomes a manager decision, then a status in the student list, then an entry in the audit trail.
  • Import creates students and login cards, which feed the student login.
  • Admin permissions decide what every other role sees and does; the audit trail records permission changes, archiving, rule changes, deletions and logins.

Recording and the Mushaf

Mistake marks. The teacher taps a verse and picks ن (forgot), ت (hesitated), خ (error) or ص (correct), with an optional note. Every mark is a new entry with date, type, teacher and note, so a verse can read: 1 Oct forgot, 8 Oct hesitated, 15 Oct correct. The verse shows its latest color and a letter, so the meaning survives without color. The student, their teachers, their parent and the kuttab manager can see it.

What is recorded. Only the teacher records, in class. Students never record at home (this replaced the plan's home recordings).

ModeWhat is keptDefault limits
Normal recitationA clip around each mark (ن ت خ): 5 seconds before and 10 after; the rest is erased; nothing is kept if there were no mistakesClip kept 90 days; session limit 20 minutes on the phone
ExamThe whole recordingMaximum 45 minutes (a hizb takes 20 to 30); kept 60 days

The manager sets the clip seconds, the exam maximum and the retention in the kuttab settings, which also shows a storage estimate.

Storage on the free plan. Recordings are stored in small pieces inside Firestore. The free quota is about 1 GB for the whole system (from a third-party guide; to confirm on Google's pages).

  • Voice at 16 kbps is about 0.12 MB a minute; the plan used 24 kbps, which is 0.18 MB a minute. We must pick one in step 0.
  • A 30-minute exam is about 3.6 MB (16 kbps) or 5.4 MB (24 kbps).
  • One exam a month for 62 students would add about 2.7 GB a year, so exam retention is short (60 days). At 90% full, new recordings pause and the rest keeps working.
  • Options not yet applied: lower exam quality, delete exam audio after grading, or let the manager export it first.

After class. The teacher reviews the clip list (play, delete with undo) and sees the upload status (waiting for connection, uploading, saved). If the microphone is blocked or storage is full, marks and attendance still work.

Live recitation tracking

The idea. As a student recites, the Mushaf on the teacher's phone highlights where they are. Two ways to build it: the teacher moves the highlight (easy, instant, free) or the app listens and moves it by itself (hard).

Decision for version 1: teacher-driven. Automatic tracking stays a later experiment.

What we tested. A page in the browser records the recitation, runs a Quran-tuned Whisper model (whisper-base-ar-quran, Apache-2.0, 4-bit files, about 160 MB), and matches the heard words to the Hafs text of the chosen surah. The text came from Tanzil (CC BY 3.0, unmodified).

Where it ranSpeed per stepResult
Test machine, CPUabout 5 to 8 sMatching scores 0.97 to 1.0 on a Sudais recording; no wrong jumps; highlight 8 to 9 s late
Your laptop, graphics cardmedian 3.6 s5 of 7 verses of Al-Fatiha; no wrong jumps; verse 3 missed by a rule I wrote (minimum 3 words)
Your phone (Android 10), CPUmedian 13.4 s2 of 7 verses; most speech fell between steps; quiet microphone

Why it is slow. The search for the verse takes under 2 ms. The model is the cost: Whisper always reads a 30-second block and then writes the text word by word. A 3-second window took 11 s on your phone, so a short surah does not help.

What Tarteel does differently. Tarteel is a purpose-built system (more than 75,000 minutes of recitation, word-level mistake detection, no public API found). I found no sign of Warsh support. We cannot reuse it; we can reuse the ideas: a small streaming model, and the known text as a constraint.

Options for faster tracking

  1. A small streaming recognizer limited to the words of the chosen surah (best chance; needs research).
  2. A stronger machine as the listener: your PC through a free tunnel, or a free Hugging Face Space. Free spaces are small and sleep.
  3. A native phone app (a separate project).
  4. Analyze the recording after class instead of live.

Warsh. A Warsh model needs Warsh audio with the exact Warsh text, expert review and a graphics card for training. The Hafs model is a starting point, since most words match. Detecting Warsh-specific mistakes (madd length, silat, naql) is a long-term goal.

Fatiha pilot (agreed, not started). Train a small model for Al-Fatiha only, using Hafs audio from EveryAyah (private research only) plus kuttab recordings with parents' permission. It needs everyayah.com allowed in the environment. We do not use YouTube: its terms forbid downloading, and videos of children carry no consent. A Fatiha-only result will flatter the idea, because it is the easiest case; a letters-based model on short surahs would tell us more.

The recitation lab and hosting

The lab is a separate test page, not part of the app. It has no database, nothing is uploaded, and the speech model runs in the browser.

  • Address: https://hifd-lab.web.app, a second Hosting site in the same Firebase project, behind a shared password (a simple lock, not real security).
  • Code: the recitation-lab folder in the repository; deployed by the same GitHub workflow (pull requests #8 and #9).
  • Features: model loading, microphone or audio-file input, a Hafs Mushaf with a moving highlight, manual correction buttons, a results file and an optional audio export, both kept on the device.
  • Tuning: the address bar accepts ?win=, ?new= and ?thr= for the window length, the minimum new audio and the thread count.
  • A rule to fix: the tracker needs at least 3 heard words, so a 2-word verse such as verse 3 of Al-Fatiha is never highlighted. A replay of your laptop run showed that allowing 2 words fixes it with no wrong jumps. The change is ready to make and not applied.
  • Risk: the lab shares the project's free hosting transfer limit, and its public files are large. Remove the lab site when testing ends.

Risks

  • Law and consent: Morocco's Law 09-08 (CNDP) governs personal data; sources say parental consent is needed for children's data. Voice recordings and the shared ranking need a consent step. Confirm with the CNDP or a lawyer.
  • V1 data: only the code was seen, never the real database. A read-only export is needed before step 9.
  • Effort: roughly 4 to 8 million tokens for v2, with wide uncertainty.

Each step ends with something you can open and test. v1 stays live throughout, and every change is a pull request you approve, then a test site, then your approval for release.

  1. Specification: exact rules, screens and data; the Warsh text source and licence; the four small tests.

  3. Accounts and kuttabs: Google login, student code and PIN, parents, separate kuttabs, groups, kuttab settings, the Permissions screen, security rules.
  4. Students and archive: enrollment, Excel import, archive with history and warnings.
  5. The teacher's day: Today screen, five attendance statuses, memorization, revision, tests, notes, scoring, offline sync.
  6. The Mushaf and marking: the Warsh book, four marks, verse history, weak-spots map, recording with the free-plan limits.
  7. Plans and notifications: planned tasks (missed ones kept), in-app notices.
